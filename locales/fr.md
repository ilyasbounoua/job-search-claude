# Locale pack — France

Read by `/setup` when the search is in France. Merge the relevant parts into `playbook.md` and `admin/legal-status.md`, in the user's chat language. **Amounts and rules change: check every figure on the official source before using it.**

## Vocabulary

- **CDI**: permanent contract. **CDD**: fixed-term. **Alternance**: work-study. **Période d'essai**: trial period (negotiable, often 3 to 4 months for engineers, renewable once).
- **ESN** (formerly SSII): IT services company that places engineers at clients. Judge each offer by the mission: real development or not, and how long.
- **Cooptation**: internal referral, often with a bonus for the employee. Ask the contact if their company has a cooptation form: it is the strongest channel.
- **Salaire brut annuel**: gross yearly salary, the number used in offers and forms ("40–45 K€"). Net is about 22–25 % lower.
- **Jeune diplômé**: new graduate. Offers saying "expérience confirmée", "3 ans minimum", "hors stage et alternance" are hard gates.

## Job boards and lists

- Welcome to the Jungle, Hellowork, LinkedIn, APEC (cadres and new graduates), Indeed, JobTeaser (schools), France Travail.
- Startup lists to find a person in, not to apply cold: Welcome to the Jungle company pages, Station F job board, the French Tech lists.
- Agencies for new graduates: many clients no longer pay agencies for juniors; measure before investing time.

## Salary norms (rough, check current data)

- New-graduate engineers: often higher in Paris than in other cities; outside Paris juniors may start lower.
- Ask the range early; never say a number first if avoidable; know the legal floor (below) before any call.

## Interviews

- Startups: often an intro call, a technical round (live coding or a take-home), a product or system-design discussion, a culture fit with founders.
- Formal "vous" with HR by default; many startups and alumni use "tu": match the contact.

## Legal status for non-EU nationals (to research and confirm at setup)

EU, EEA and Swiss citizens have free access to work: write it in one line and move on.

For other nationalities, research on **service-public.fr**, **administration-etrangers-en-france.interieur.gouv.fr** (ANEF), **welcometofrance.com** and the user's **préfecture** website:

- **Student permit → work after the degree:** the right to work while a student is limited (about 60 % of full time). After a master-level degree, the main routes are usually:
  - **Carte « recherche d'emploi ou création d'entreprise »** (often called APS): a temporary permit to look for a job after graduation, which allows working.
  - **Carte « talent – salarié qualifié »**: needs a salary above a yearly threshold (indexed every year: check the current amount) and a degree; no work authorisation from the employer.
  - **Carte « salarié »**: the employer requests a **work authorisation** on ANEF; for a job related to the degree and above a monthly threshold, the employment situation may not be opposable. Check the current conditions.
- **Bilateral agreements** (e.g. with Algeria, Morocco, Tunisia and others) can change the rules: always check the agreement for the user's nationality.
- Prolongation receipts (récépissés, attestations de prolongation d'instruction) keep the rights of the previous permit: say "je suis en règle jusqu'au …", never "mon titre est valable".
- The competent préfecture depends on the home address, and each préfecture has its own procedure (ANEF, démarches-simplifiées, appointments).
- Support organisations: La Cimade, ADATE, the school's international office.

Write in `admin/legal-status.md`: the route chosen, the salary floor it implies (gross yearly and monthly), when to tell the employer, the sentence for recruiters, the questions for the préfecture, and the sources.
